A guiding idea

The Bridge Principle

What we receive. What we make of it. What we pass forward.

We are responsible for what passes through our hands—and for what becomes possible after us.

That is the idea at the heart of The Bridge Principle.

We enter a world already shaped by other people. Someone taught the skill, kept the story, cared for the place, or made the opportunity possible. Some of what we receive is strong. Some needs repair. Our part begins with paying attention.

“not simply preserving what we've inherited, but helping others create something worth passing forward.”

Understand what you have received.

Before changing something, take the time to understand it. Who built it? What purpose did it serve? What knowledge would disappear if no one stopped to ask?

Respect for the past includes honest judgment. We can preserve what is valuable while acknowledging what needs to change.

Put care into the work in front of you.

Responsibility becomes visible in ordinary decisions: a repair made carefully, a promise kept, a source checked, a lesson written down while it is still fresh.

Good judgment has to become something another person can use. A system should make the next decision clearer. A book should give its reader something worth returning to. A preserved story should help a life remain more than a name.

Leave room for someone else to build.

The work should give others a stronger place to begin. Share the method. Explain the reasoning. Make room for their judgment and for work you could not have imagined yourself.

This is part of what draws me to publishing: the possibility of helping another author bring worthwhile work into the world.

I think of the bridge as a connection between what we receive, what we do with it, and what we make possible for others. It is a standard I want to practice in my work and my writing.

The question I return to is simple: What will someone else be able to carry forward because I took care with this?
